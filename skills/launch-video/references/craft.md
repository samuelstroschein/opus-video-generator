# Craft: what makes a launch video look expensive

Principles adapted from @twoclipping's public "launch videos with Opus" workflow, plus lessons from our own runs. The model's defaults (centered text, gradient background, everything fading in) are what make videos look like templates. Push against them on purpose.

## Seven things that read as expensive

1. **Nothing fades in; things change shape.** A button grows into the next page. Text rises out of a mask line. A color floods out of one object and shrinks back into another. Avoid crossfades and blur-ins.
2. **Few cuts; each scene comes out of the previous one.** Carry one element across a boundary (a shared element) instead of cutting.
3. **Something happens on every beat.** Dead time reads as amateur. No hold longer than about one second unless it is the point.
4. **Things settle with a small bounce** when they land (a spring with slight overshoot), like real objects. Never bouncy cartoon easing.
5. **One camera move at a time.** Never zoom in and out back to back, never reverse direction. Each scene gets one eased move.
6. **Real sound** for every click and whoosh (not available yet in this app; design the beats so audio can be laid on later).
7. **Check your own work** before the user watches: look at frames, find the cramped, cut-off or unreadable ones, fix them.

## Style

- **Default to 2D, light and minimal** unless the brand is clearly dark: warm white canvas, black UI, one accent color, one clean font (Geist or Inter). That reads premium. Dark 3D-ish "game trailer" looks (glows, particles) read cheap by default.
- Dark is fine when it is the brand: then be precise (flat surfaces, thin borders, one accent), not glowy.
- One accent color. One typeface family plus at most one display face for the product name. Large type: headlines 90–180px on a 1920 canvas, UI labels not below 24px.
- Generous spacing, a clear focal point per scene, real hierarchy. Never a generic card grid.

## Banned

Crossfades, blur-ins, particle bursts, 3D flips, camera moves that reverse, holds longer than a second, mismatched icon strokes, placeholder boxes in the product UI, lorem ipsum, anything that looks like a template.

## Drawing the product UI

Invented UI is the quickest way to look cheap: wrong spacing, fake-looking buttons, placeholder data. When you must draw it: use the product's real colors and font from the brief; real, specific copy and plausible data (ids, names, counts); consistent row height, padding and radius; the product's real component shapes (what its buttons, chips and lists actually look like). Draw less of it, larger, rather than a dense mock.

## Process and how-to videos

Show where the viewer is in the process: a step rail along the bottom (done, now, next) that stays on screen for the whole video, and one large card for the current quantity or state ("1 OZ · CAMPARI", "STEP 3 OF 6") that ticks as it changes, with a small bar for the amount. One gesture per step, and its label appears exactly when the action happens. Keep the one hero object (the glass, the editor window) identical in every scene, and let only its contents change. A labelled level line or ruler next to a vessel makes amounts readable at a glance.

## Text

On-screen text is short: a headline of 2–7 words per beat, in the product's voice. Reveal word by word or line by line on a mask. Never fade a paragraph.
